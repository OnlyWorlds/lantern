OnlyWorlds on a phone · design for the Captain · working name

Lantern

Your worlds in your pocket. Find anything, follow its links, fix a field, catch an idea in seconds, and run a game from your phone at the table. It works without signal and sends your changes when it can.

Atlas is the studio, where you build. Lantern is what you carry. They share the same worlds through your OnlyWorlds account, so a change on one shows up on the other.

The shape: a world browser

Lantern opens to a list of every world you have, the way a phone's notes app opens to your notes. Tap one and you are inside it; switch any time from the top. There are three kinds of world on that list, and you can tell them apart at a glance:

Online worlds
The worlds in your OnlyWorlds account, yours and the ones shared with you. Atlas, Lantern and every other tool see the same world. The phone keeps a copy so it opens on a train.
Worlds on this phone
A world that lives only here: started on the phone, or opened from a world file. No account needed. Lantern keeps reminding you to save a copy, and one tap puts it online.
Moppetopia
The demo world, one tap from the first screen, to see what Lantern does before you sign up for anything.

And two kinds of people use it. The one who builds the world, with everything. And, for a game, the players: each on their own phone, invited into the world as a player, each seeing only what their character knows. Lantern is the game master's pocket and the players' too.

What it does

Find and read
Search as you type, or say it. Every element with its filled fields, its links as chips you can follow, and everything that links back to it. Maps with their pins.
Capture
One button on every screen. A name and a line, typed or spoken, or a photo, saved in a few seconds. On Android, once Lantern is installed, share text from any app straight into your world.
Edit anywhere
Change any field, with or without signal. Edits wait in a queue and send themselves. If someone changed the same field elsewhere, Lantern shows both and asks.
At the table
A game master's mode: the session's cast pinned, lookup by voice, and a secret given to the party with one tap: it appears on each player's phone. Players without an account scan a page instead.
Ask your world
Ask a question in plain words and get an answer built only from your world, with every element it used shown as a chip you can open. Paid from free daily tokens.
After the session
The notes you caught during play become proposed changes: a new character, what the party learned, an event. You tick what is true; the rest is dropped.
As they know it
Read the world as the party knows it: only the elements their characters are named on. Your players see exactly that on their own phones, live, joined by your invite.
Hand it on
Open the same element in Atlas on your laptop, or send a world file to a friend. Lantern never locks a world in.

Six stories (the screens to the right)

  1. Someone hears about OnlyWorlds. They open lantern.onlyworlds.com on their phone and see three doors (screen 1). They tap Moppetopia and are reading a world in two seconds, with no account. The first screen asks them to pin Lantern to their home screen, and they do. Later they start a world of their own on the phone (it lives only there, and Lantern says so), and a week on they tap "Put it online" and make an account.
  2. On the train, no signal. You open Lantern; your worlds are there (screen 2). In The Drowned Bells you search "harbour" (screen 3), open Ada Vell (screen 4), and change her title. The edit shows "waiting to send". At the station the phone finds signal and the edit lands; Atlas on your laptop shows it the next time it syncs.
  3. An idea at the bus stop. Anywhere in the app you tap the lantern button (screen 5). It already offers Character, the type you used last. You say "a ferryman who refuses to cross at night" into the keyboard's microphone, tap Save, and put the phone away. Four seconds.
  4. Two edits meet. You changed Ada's title on the train; that afternoon you had changed it in Atlas too. When the phone sends, OnlyWorlds refuses it, because the element moved. Lantern sees that the other change touched a different field and re-sends by itself. Only when the same field changed in both places does it ask: keep mine, or take theirs (screen 6).
  5. Game night. You start a session for The Drowned Bells (screen 7): tonight's cast is pinned at the top. A player asks who runs the bell tower; you tap the lookup and say "bell tower" into the keyboard's mic. When the party finds the letter, you tap Give it to the party (screen 8): it appears on Mira's phone and the others' (screen 13), and your world now records that they know it. The friend who came without an account scans tonight's page. Notes you jot during play are caught to the session.
  6. The morning after. Lantern turns last night's notes into changes to the world (screen 10): the new innkeeper, the fact the party learned, the event at the docks. You tick what is true. Later you ask "who in Tharn could know the bell's secret?" (screen 9) and get an answer from your own world, its sources shown.

Your question: account only, or open?

A · Account only
Every world comes from an OnlyWorlds account; Moppetopia is the only way in without one. One truth (onlyworlds.com), one kind of world, the simplest app to explain.
Costs: a stranger must sign up before writing a single line. "Your world is a folder" has no phone answer. A world file from a friend can't be opened without an account.
B · Open, the account is how worlds travel (lean)
Start a world on the phone or open a world file, no account. Sign in when you want the same world on Atlas or a second device. The phone's copy of an online world and a world that lives only on the phone are the same storage, so this costs little extra code.
Costs: Safari can wipe a phone-only world on an iPhone if the page is not pinned and goes unused. So a phone-only world carries a visible "save a copy" and a one-tap "Put it online", and Lantern asks the phone to keep its storage once it is pinned.

Why the lean moved from A: I chose A when we were building in small versions, so the phone-only world could come later. As one full product, B lets people start without signing up and keeps the folder promise, and the risk has a clear answer. Skeld's review adds the players: a player joins by invite and an account, a stranger reads the demo or starts a world without one, and the account is how any of it travels.

Works without signal: opening Lantern itself, reading, searching, editing, capturing (photos too: they wait in the queue like edits), the table's lookup by the keyboard's mic, and the lens. Needs signal, and says so on the button: signing in, revealing, asking, and putting a world online. A read-only world has no camera.

One product, built in an order

One v1.0, designed whole, as you asked. It is built in this order, and each part is tried with real taps on an iPhone and an Android phone before the next starts: the doors and the worlds list · find and read · capture · Lantern itself offline (airplane mode, a cold start, a world opens) · edit with the queue and conflicts · phone-only worlds and world files · maps and the knowledge lens · the table and reveal · ask and the morning after (as the token proxy lands).

Built from what the ecosystem already has, not beside it: the OnlyWorlds SDK 4.9 (only-if-unchanged writes, the change feed, photo upload, links, ids made offline), a world-file reader shared with Atlas inside the SDK (Kael), and the house colours Atlas and the docs use. The look on these screens is a sketch. Boss's notes from running the shelf, our one phone surface in daily use, come before the first line.

Not in Lantern: drawing maps, charts, the writing room, building pages. That is Atlas. On a phone, Atlas will show a door to Lantern with "continue to Atlas" under it.
